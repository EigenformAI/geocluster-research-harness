## 1. Introduction

This report summarizes the results of the 2024 surface and shallow drill
sampling program at the fictional Meridian Ridge Prospect. A total of 240
samples were collected across five mapped lithological units and assayed for
gold, base metals, and pathfinder elements. The accompanying dataset is
provided in `data/meridian_ridge_geochem.csv`.

## 2. Geological Setting

The prospect is underlain by a folded sequence of basaltic to andesitic
volcanics intruded by a granodiorite stock. Late-stage quartz veining follows
the contact between the andesite unit and the intrusive body, striking roughly
north-northeast. A weakly developed schist belt bounds the prospect to the
west.

Mapped units sampled in this program:

| Unit | Samples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Basalt | ~70 | Background geochemistry |
| Andesite | ~50 | Locally silicified near vein contacts |
| Granodiorite | ~50 | Fresh to weakly altered |
| Quartz vein | ~35 | Main mineralized structure |
| Schist | ~35 | Western margin |

## 3. Sampling and Analytical Method

Samples were collected on a nominal 100 m grid with infill at 50 m over the
vein corridor, from surface to a maximum vertical depth of 220 m. Gold was
determined by fire assay (detection limit 0.005 ppm; values below detection
are reported as `<0.005`). Base metals and pathfinders were determined by
four-acid digest ICP-MS.

## 4. Results

Key observations from the assay data:

- Gold values are strongly associated with the **quartz vein** unit and, to a
  lesser degree, with adjacent **andesite**, consistent with a structurally
  controlled epithermal system.
- Copper and arsenic form a coherent pathfinder halo around the vein corridor;
  arsenic in particular extends 100–200 m into the andesite hanging wall.
- Lead and zinc show no systematic spatial association with gold and are
  interpreted as background lithological variation.
- The granodiorite is geochemically unremarkable apart from weakly elevated
  copper at its northern contact.

## 5. Conclusions and Recommendations

The 2024 program supports a structurally controlled precious-metal target
along the andesite–granodiorite contact. Recommended follow-up:

1. Cluster analysis of the multi-element data to formalize the geochemical
   domains described above.
2. Anomaly ranking of Au–Cu–As within the vein corridor.
3. Three diamond drill holes testing the corridor at 150 m depth beneath the
   highest-tenor surface samples.
